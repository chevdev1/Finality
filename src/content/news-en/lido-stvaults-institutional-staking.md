## What stVaults are

stVaults are Lido V3's modular staking infrastructure, live since the V3 mainnet launch on January 30, 2026. The idea is to separate the liquidity pool (what gives stETH its liquidity) from the operating parameters of a specific vault (who the custodian is, who runs the validators, what the fees are) — an institutional client gets control over the latter without losing access to the former.

## What exactly launched on September 9

Stakely, an infrastructure provider that has run validators since 2020, announced two products on top of stVaults. The first is a public vault open to any user: ETH is deposited through Stakely's own interface, automatically staked and directed into EarnETH, and in return the participant receives an ERC-20 token representing their share. The second is dedicated, non-custodial institutional vaults, where the client — an asset manager, ETF/ETP issuer, DAO, or corporate treasury — sets its own custody, access-rights, fee, and liquidity parameters, and assets of different clients are not mixed together.

## Not Lido's first institutional vault

Earlier, on June 12, 2026, Lido had already launched a similar institutional product together with Luganodes — there institutions keep full control of withdrawal credentials in a non-custodial arrangement, and the target audience is the same asset managers, ETF issuers, and corporate treasuries. The September launch with Stakely extends the same principle to a second partner rather than replacing the earlier one.

## Why it matters for the reader

For a protocol of Lido's scale — the largest by staked ETH — expanding institutional vaults means a more direct channel for large, regulated capital to enter staking, bypassing direct interaction with the public stETH pool. The practical question for a DeFi observer: does the actual decentralization of Lido's validators grow with it, or are institutional vaults simply adding a more convenient wrapper around the same set of operators.
