## What was signed

On August 31 – September 1, G20 finance ministers and central bank governors met in Asheville, North Carolina. The meeting produced a joint chair's statement in which countries committed to developing "responsible and effective regulatory and supervisory frameworks" for digital assets — with no link to specific rules or an implementation calendar.

## What's actually new

The document recognizes the transformative role of digital financial innovation, including digital assets, for broad economic growth, and promises to build "clear pathways" for responsible innovation in the area, taking into account cross-border opportunities and risks. The G20 is also awaiting the Financial Stability Board's final report on the cross-border implications of global stablecoin arrangements and sources of data on them, and asked the FATF to make sure jurisdictions with significant virtual-asset turnover comply with the organization's standards.

## Why the details are missing again

The statement contains no binding commitments or specific compliance deadlines — implementation is left to national regulators and the relevant international organizations. For the market, it's another signal of agreement at the level of rhetoric without any change to the actual rules of the game in individual countries.
