## What the rule proposes

Regulation Crypto Assets builds on a foundation laid on March 17, 2026, when the SEC issued a 68-page interpretive release clarifying how federal securities laws apply to crypto-asset activity. The new rule adds to that base a separate, tailored offering path specifically for investment contracts based on crypto assets — that is, for cases where the asset is not itself a security but a specific transaction in it creates the hallmarks of an investment contract.

## Five categories instead of one

The SEC's classification framework divides digital assets into five categories: digital commodities, digital collectibles, digital tools, stablecoins, and digital securities. Only the last category is treated as a security by default — the other four can become the subject of an investment contract depending on exactly how they are offered and sold, but are not securities automatically.

## What this changes for the market

The approach contrasts with the era of enforcement through lawsuits against the biggest platforms for operating without registration: now the SEC, together with the CFTC, is publishing classification guidance and forming a rulemaking working group instead of litigating, reserving lawsuits for cases of outright fraud. For the industry, that means more predictability — but only if the CLARITY bill dividing oversight between the SEC and CFTC is also passed by Congress.
