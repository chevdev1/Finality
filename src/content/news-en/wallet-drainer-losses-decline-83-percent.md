## A rare piece of good news, with a caveat

Most crypto-security stories Finality covers are about rising losses: $3.6 billion over a year and a half from hacks, key compromise overtaking code bugs as the main cause of DeFi losses. Against that backdrop, the wallet-drainer statistics are a rare exception: a specific, measurable type of fraud really is declining, rather than just going quiet before a new surge.

## How drainers work and why the numbers matter

Wallet drainers don't require the victim to hand over a private key directly — instead they trick people into signing a malicious smart-contract permission through a cloned decentralized application or a fake wallet-connect window, gaining the right to move the victim's tokens. The drop in losses from $494 million to $83.85 million and in affected wallets from 332,000+ to 106,106 is a decline of almost an order of magnitude on both measures at once, which points to a real effect (for example, better warnings in wallets and user awareness) rather than statistical chance.

## The drainer industry hasn't gone away

An important caveat: drainer kits themselves keep being sold and rented on underground forums, bundled with ready-made phishing pages, wallet-connect integration, and dashboards for tracking victims. The decline in losses doesn't mean the attack infrastructure has disappeared — it may mean users and platforms adapted faster than attackers can compensate through greater attack volume, but the threat itself remains active.

## The overall picture hasn't improved

The decline applies specifically to this category of fraud, not to crypto crime as a whole: in one source's wording, an exchange or protocol hack "has stopped being a rare catastrophe and become a routine market risk." That fits what Finality has already recorded in its coverage of the record number of price-manipulation attacks on DeFi lenders and of the $292 million KelpDAO bridge hack — large structural attacks on infrastructure and protocols keep growing, even as one specific type of retail fraud declines.
