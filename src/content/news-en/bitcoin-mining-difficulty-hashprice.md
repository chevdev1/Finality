## What's behind the drop in difficulty

Mining difficulty automatically adjusts to the network's combined computing power: when some miners switch off equipment or move it to other tasks, difficulty falls so that the average block time stays around 10 minutes. A 19.3% drop from the October 2025 peak is one of the largest difficulty declines of 2026, which points to some capacity leaving the network or being redirected.

## Why miner revenue is rising anyway

Despite the lower difficulty, [hashprice](/en/glossary/#hashprice) — the standard measure of revenue per unit of computing power — rose over 30 days from $32.42 to $39.63 per PH/s per day. The combination of lower difficulty and a stable or rising bitcoin price means the miners who remain on the network earn more revenue on the same amount of hardware — a rare combination that the market usually smooths out fairly quickly.

## Where the capacity is going

Some mining companies are redirecting hardware and capital toward computing for artificial intelligence, where revenue per megawatt is often higher than from mining bitcoin at current difficulty and price — a trend that may explain part of the hashrate decline without a corresponding fall in the BTC price.
