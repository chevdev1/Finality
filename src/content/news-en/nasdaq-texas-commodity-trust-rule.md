## What changed formally

The amendment to Nasdaq Texas Rule 5711(d) is a narrow but telling technical step: instead of a case-by-case abstract analysis through the Howey test, the rule directly lists bitcoin, ether, solana, and XRP as assets meeting the exchange's commodity-trust standards. This differs from the SEC's general classification framework (five categories of digital assets), which describes types of assets rather than naming specific coins at the level of an individual exchange's rules.

## Why it matters beyond the formalities

Naming specific assets in an exchange rule reduces uncertainty for structurers of trust products on that particular venue — they no longer need to prove on their own that each asset meets the exchange's criteria. It's a small but concrete example of how the SEC's classification framework is gradually working its way down to the level of practical exchange rules, rather than remaining only a high-level document.

## What remains unresolved

The decision applies only to the rules of a specific exchange (Nasdaq Texas) and isn't an industry-wide precedent for all US trading venues. The broader framework — passage of the CLARITY bill dividing oversight between the SEC and CFTC — remains unresolved and is being discussed separately.
