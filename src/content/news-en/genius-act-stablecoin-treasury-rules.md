## What the proposed rule settles

The GENIUS Act, signed by the president in July 2025, became the first US federal law specifically about crypto, but left key terms open — what exactly counts as "issuing" or "selling" a stablecoin. The proposed rule (Notice of Proposed Rulemaking) published on August 18 closes those gaps and creates a "safe harbor" — conditions under which a market participant definitely doesn't fall under the licensing requirement.

## Effective dates

From the date the law takes effect — January 18, 2027 — issuing a payment stablecoin in the US without the appropriate federal or state license becomes illegal. A separate, later ban takes effect on July 18, 2028: it will close to unlicensed digital-asset providers the ability to offer or sell stablecoins to persons in the US.

## What's next

The public comment period on the proposed rule is now open and ends on October 19, 2026. That is the window in which banks and stablecoin issuers, including U.S. Bank with its USBDC pilot, will try to influence the final wording of the licensing requirements.
