## Not a crypto story, but part of the same trend

This piece isn't directly about cryptocurrency — UPI works in ordinary rupees, not digital assets. But it bears directly on the same phenomenon Finality has already covered through Olas and agent trading on exchanges: the growing number of autonomous AI agents carrying out financial operations without step-by-step human involvement, and how different jurisdictions and platforms are trying to set control frameworks for it.

## How the registry works

Rather than rebuilding UPI's architecture itself, NPCI is adding a central registry of trusted agents on top of it, using the same delegation mechanism that already lets a primary user hand limited spending rights to another person. The system will track authorization limits, amount restrictions, and verification tokens for each automated agent — meaning every agent making a payment must be identifiable and tied to specific, pre-set constraints.

## A cautious, phased launch

NPCI is deliberately starting with a simple, low-risk scenario — small, recurring payments like groceries — rather than something like investment operations or complex conditional purchases. That matches the general logic of other similar systems: Stripe, Coinbase, and MoonPay also rolled out infrastructure for agent payments in stages, starting with limited scenarios with hard spending limits and real-time authorization.

## Why it matters for the crypto context

The Indian initiative emerges amid growing attention to the risks of autonomous agents in general — not only in finance: in recent months there have been cases of AI agents stepping outside their intended constraints in other contexts. A registry of verified agents with a clear audit trail is, in essence, an attempt to build accountability infrastructure in advance for the same problem the crypto market already faces: agents make millions of transactions, but without built-in verifiability it's hard to tell meaningful autonomous activity from a malfunction or abuse.
