## What the September 15 vote decides

The CLARITY bill is meant to divide oversight of the crypto market between the SEC and the CFTC — a question still unresolved in the US and one over which regulators have clashed on jurisdiction for years. The September 15 vote is not final passage but a cloture vote: a test of whether Senate leadership has the 60 votes needed to end debate and bring the bill to a final vote without the risk of a [filibuster](/en/glossary/#filibuster).

## What's holding up agreement on the text

Three questions remain open: ethics disclosure rules for lawmakers who own digital assets; rules against illicit financing; and whether stablecoin issuers may pay holders rewards resembling yield — an issue that at the last moment reignited disputes even among the bill's supporters.

## Meanwhile, at the SEC

On August 21, the SEC published in the Federal Register a proposed Regulation Crypto Assets rule, creating a separate offering pathway for certain investment contracts based on crypto assets — an attempt by the regulator to move independently of whether CLARITY passes in Congress.
