**What changed.** Since the start of 2026, DeFi protocols have lost at least $1.3 billion to exploits, according to combined data from Forbes and analytics firm CertiK. More important than the amount itself is that, for the first time in the history of tracking, private-key compromise has overtaken smart-contract bugs as the main cause of losses.

## Why this is fundamental

For years the industry treated code audits as the main security barrier: the cleaner the audit, the safer the protocol. The shift toward key theft — through phishing of privileged operators, compromise of multisig wallets, or social engineering — means that even flawless code doesn't protect if the person or process behind transaction signing is compromised.

## What this means for auditors

More and more audit teams are including operational security in their reviews: who holds the keys, how they're stored, whether hardware security modules are used, and what happens if one of a multisig's signers is compromised. The September 6 Liquid Network incident — although formally a bug in cache code rather than a stolen key — shows the same trend from another side: the consequences of a single vulnerability in critical infrastructure can be many times larger than any phishing attack.
