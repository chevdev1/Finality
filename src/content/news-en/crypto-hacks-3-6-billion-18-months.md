## An audit doesn't protect against modern attacks

The study's key finding: having passed a security audit barely correlates with resilience to real attacks. 88% of stolen funds came from platforms that had formally passed an independent security review, and about 60% of the affected venues had such an audit at all. That means attackers are aiming at areas a typical smart-contract code audit doesn't examine.

## Where the attack vector has shifted

Most incidents are the result of cyberattacks on infrastructure and the theft of passkeys (hardware or software access keys), not the exploitation of vulnerabilities in a protocol's public code. This is consistent with earlier Forbes and CertiK data showing that key compromise overtook smart-contract bugs as the main cause of DeFi losses in 2026 for the first time.

## What this means for security vetting

For the reader, the practical takeaway: an "audit from a well-known firm" on a protocol's website is no guarantee of protection against key theft, operator phishing, or compromise of storage infrastructure. That's why, in its verification log, Finality distinguishes on-chain data confirmation, the counterparty's official statement, and independent review — "there was an audit" isn't on that list as a standalone guarantee.
