## What the three directions mean

The Privacy Stewards roadmap describes three interrelated tasks: private writes (transactions that hide the details of an operation), private reads (accessing network state without revealing exactly what the user is reading), and private proofs (the ability to confirm that an action was valid without revealing its contents). The end goal is to make private on-chain actions on Ethereum cheap and ubiquitous, while remaining compatible with regulatory compliance requirements rather than anonymous in the mixer sense.

## What's already being tested

The public Ethrex testnet currently supports several key proposals (EIPs) from this roadmap: EIP-8141 Frame Transactions, EIP-8250 Keyed Nonces, EIP-8272 Recent Roots, FOCIL inclusion lists (EIP-7805), and VOPS validation profiles (EIP-8369). It is the first public infrastructure where elements of the privacy plan can be tested in practice rather than only on paper.

## How it fits with the rest of the roadmap

Privacy isn't the only priority: the Glamsterdam upgrade, with ePBS and a 200 million gas limit, is expected to activate in the third quarter, and the next step will be preparing the Hegotá upgrade (late 2026 to early 2027), where privacy, censorship resistance, and account abstraction at the protocol level are priorities, including work on post-quantum protection of the network.
