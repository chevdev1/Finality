## Insurance against a failed vote

The Senate is due to hold a procedural vote on the CLARITY bill on September 15 — it needs 60 votes to advance. With the outcome still undecided, CFTC Chairman Michael Selig has decided not to wait for the result: he has directed the agency's staff to begin working out crypto market-structure rules based on authority the CFTC already has, without waiting for a new law.

## What the plan is based on

Selig's approach rests on the CFTC's existing oversight of derivatives, futures, and swaps tied to digital assets, plus its powers to pursue fraud in the spot commodity market. It is not a full replacement for the CLARITY bill, which is meant to close a broader regulatory gap (above all the status of the spot market for crypto commodities, where the CFTC has limited jurisdiction today), but a way to get at least some structure of rules without waiting for Congress.

## The regulator's own position

Selig publicly stresses that the legislative route through CLARITY remains his preferred option — working in parallel on rules under existing authority doesn't mean abandoning the bill. It's more a signal to the market and the Senate: the agency won't wait indefinitely for a legislative decision if the process stalls.

## Why it matters

For crypto market participants, the difference between "rules under CLARITY" and "CFTC rules under current authority" is practically felt: the latter would almost certainly be narrower in scope (it wouldn't settle the spot-market question the way a full law would) and could be revised by the next administration far more easily than an act of Congress. It would be a temporary structure, not a permanent one — but it may turn out to be the only one if the September 15 vote fails to gather the 60 votes needed.
