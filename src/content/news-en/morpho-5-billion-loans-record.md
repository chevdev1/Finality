## A record against a narrowing gap with the leader

Morpho, a lending protocol built on an isolated-markets model (as opposed to Aave's single pool), crossed $5 billion in outstanding loans on September 1 — a record for the protocol. Aave remains larger in absolute active loans ($12.6 billion versus Morpho's $4.83 billion), but the relative gap is narrowing, especially after Morpho held a funding round in June.

## Stablecoins dominate the loan mix

95% of loans on Morpho are denominated in stablecoins, 62% of them specifically in USDC. That suggests the protocol's main use case isn't speculative lending against volatile assets but more conservative operations: borrowing stable liquidity against other collateral, typical of institutional and semi-institutional users rather than retail traders chasing yield.

## Expansion through RWA and new ecosystems

Two specific developments underline where the protocol is heading. First, Morpho Blue became the leading lending market on Robinhood Chain, the network launched by the broker Robinhood — that is, the protocol is taking hold in new, not purely crypto-native ecosystems. Second, RedStone enabled instant exit from tokenized funds, allowing HYB fund shares to be used as collateral directly on Morpho — another channel of inflows of collateral backed by real assets rather than only cryptocurrencies.

## Not all smooth: $36 million in liquidations in August

In August, positions worth $36 million were liquidated on Morpho markets using PT-reUSD as collateral — but, by available data, that wasn't the result of an exploit or a bug: one market participant bought up enough YT tokens to move the PT price by 2.8% and trigger a cascade of liquidations. That distinguishes the incident from stories like the KelpDAO bridge hack — here a legitimate, if aggressive, market maneuver was at work, not a compromise of infrastructure.
