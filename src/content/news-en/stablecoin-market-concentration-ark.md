## The market has grown but become less diverse

ARK Invest analyst Lorenzo Valente pointed to a structural shift that's easy to miss if you only watch the market's total size: the stablecoin market has grown many times over in recent years, yet the concentration of capital in the hands of two issuers has increased, not decreased. In 2022, four stablecoins exceeded the $10 billion market-cap threshold; now only Tether and Circle do.

## The numbers as of mid-September

As of September 10, the total stablecoin market capitalization is $302.8 billion, up 0.37% over the week. Tether leads with a market cap of about $183.4 billion (60.57% of the market), Circle is second with $74.2–74.5 billion. Together the two issuers control about 85% of the whole market — a share that makes any regulatory or operational event around either one a systemic risk for the entire industry, not one company's problem.

## Why it matters against the backdrop of the bank consortium

This finding is best read alongside the recently announced 21-bank consortium (Goldman Sachs, Citi, Bank of America, and others) preparing its own dollar stablecoin by 2027: high market concentration around Tether and Circle is exactly the situation the bank consortium is aiming at by offering an alternative with the names of the largest regulated institutions. The question is whether the new institutional player will actually dilute the concentration or simply add a third name to the list of those holding the overwhelming majority of the market.

## What this means for the reader

High market concentration means that risks specific to a single issuer — reserve composition, audit quality, regulatory jurisdiction — become a risk for the entire stablecoin ecosystem rather than one product. That's exactly why Finality pays separate attention to events like the first full audit of USDT reserves by KPMG: for an asset that is de facto the industry's infrastructure, reserve transparency is not a secondary matter.
