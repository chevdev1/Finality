## What the vote showed

On September 15, the Senate held a procedural vote (a cloture vote) on the CLARITY bill — the one meant to divide oversight of the crypto market between the SEC and the CFTC. Cloture requires 60 "yes" votes to end debate and head off a [filibuster](/en/glossary/#filibuster); that majority couldn't be assembled. The day before, on Sunday, Republican leadership released a revised version of the bill with additional ethics restrictions — a response to Democrats' concerns that public officials could profit personally from crypto ventures. The concessions weren't enough.

## Discrepancy in the vote count

Here outlets report different numbers. The Crypto Times, citing the "official floor record," reports a tally of 49 yes to 50 no. CoinGape gives an "unofficial count" of 46 to 43. CoinDesk's live coverage limited itself to the wording "more than 40 senators voted against" — without giving an exact number on either side. What all sources share: fewer than 60 votes were gathered, and the gap wasn't on the margin but a comfortable distance away. Finality can't currently name a single correct figure and records the discrepancy as it stands rather than arbitrarily picking one version.

## Who voted against

Both sources that name senators list the same Democrats who voted "no" despite taking part in negotiations on the bill: Kirsten Gillibrand, Mark Warner, Cory Booker, Raphael Warnock, Ruben Gallego, Angela Alsobrooks, and Catherine Cortez Masto.

## What's next

Formally, the path to reconsideration isn't closed — a procedural motion to reconsider the vote has been filed, and the bill itself remains on the Senate calendar. But to bring it back to a vote, Majority Leader Thune would have to refile cloture and rebuild the coalition of votes from scratch. According to Senator Lummis, the bill won't return to the floor this year; comprehensive crypto regulation reform, by the assessment of several outlets, is put off at least until the midterm elections, with a chance of revival only in the "lame duck" session — the session of Congress after the elections but before the new Congress takes office.

## Market reaction

Bitcoin, trading overnight before the vote around $79,530, fell to $75,850–76,300 by the time the result was announced — a drop of about 4% over the day and about 3% from the intraday high. Crypto-company stocks reacted more sharply: Coinbase (COIN) lost 6.7%, Circle (CRCL) 8%, Bullish (BLSH) 4.6%, and Robinhood 3.6%.
