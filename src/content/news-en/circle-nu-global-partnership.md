## The scale of distribution

140 million customers is an order of magnitude more than the user base of most individual crypto exchanges or wallets, and that is what makes the partnership significant: integrating USDC and EURC into an existing payment service of this scale means potential stablecoin access for an audience that has probably never opened a crypto exchange or thought about holding digital assets directly.

## How it's supposed to work

Nu Global is an international payments service offering multi-currency accounts in more than 35 countries. Adding USDC and EURC as available balances within this infrastructure is positioned as a way to speed up and cheapen cross-border transfers compared with traditional banking rails — the same argument already made in Finality's coverage of Visa's stablecoin settlement and the tokenized deposits of Singaporean banks on the Swift ledger: traditional international transfers are slow and expensive precisely because of the chain of correspondent banks, and stablecoin rails offer a way around that chain.

## Timing alongside Arc

The partnership with Nu Global was announced almost simultaneously with the upcoming September 16 launch of the Circle Arc public mainnet — a specialized blockchain for stablecoin settlement. That's not a coincidence of timing but the visible part of the same strategy: Circle is simultaneously growing consumer distribution of its stablecoins through partners like Nu Global and building the infrastructure layer on which those same stablecoins are to settle in the future.

## What isn't known yet

As with most such partnerships, the announcement describes intent and technical capability, not current usage volumes — how many of Nu Global's 140 million customers will actually start holding USDC/EURC balances, and whether that translates into a measurable cut in the cost or time of a cross-border transfer for the end user, will only become clear as the feature actually rolls out, not at the announcement stage.
