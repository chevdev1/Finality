## Not just a big inflow, but the concentration of an entire market segment

$348 million in net inflows over 30 days makes Solana the leader among tracked networks on this measure for the period — ahead even of Ethereum, historically the main venue for institutional asset tokenization. But the more telling figure isn't the inflow itself but the market share: decentralized exchanges on Solana handle 95–97% of all global tokenized-stock trading volume via DEX. That's no longer a lead with a margin, but effectively a monopoly of the segment.

## Where the dominance came from

The key moment explaining this concentration is Franklin Templeton's launch of hundreds of tokenized US stocks and ETFs on Solana in January 2026. An asset manager of that scale choosing a specific network to launch a broad product line at once creates a critical mass of liquidity that competing networks then struggle to catch up to — traders and market makers go where there is already market depth, making the lead self-reinforcing.

## Scale in absolute numbers

Total tokenized assets on Solana reached roughly $4.23 billion across 398,644 holder addresses — a notable rise in the number of unique holders, not just concentration of capital in a few large players. That fits the broader picture Finality has already described in its report on the $60 billion RWA market: growth in tokenized stocks (33x in eight months, per that piece) remains one of the fastest-growing directions in the whole RWA category, and Solana has now become the main venue where that growth physically happens.

## What to keep watching

Such concentration — 95–97% of volume on a single network — is itself a risk: if Solana runs into network-stability problems (the network has faced outages before) or Franklin Templeton runs into problems with the product itself, the effect would be felt disproportionately across the entire tokenized-stock segment, not just at one issuer.
