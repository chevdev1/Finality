## Mining economics no longer add up

Public bitcoin miners, by the industry's own estimates, lose around $19,000 on every coin mined at current network difficulty and electricity prices. That is the backdrop against which the largest public mining companies are, one after another, announcing a pivot to leasing computing capacity for AI and high-performance computing — a market where margins currently look more durable than in mining BTC.

## Who sold, and how much

Core Scientific sold about 1,900 BTC (roughly $175 million at the price at the time of sale) in January and said it plans to liquidate almost all of its remaining reserve in the first quarter of 2026. Bitdeer brought its BTC treasury to zero in February. Riot Platforms sold 1,818 BTC (about $162 million) in December. The smaller Hyperscale Data sold around 100 BTC (~$6.5 million) on July 30 — not a big trade in itself, but one more data point in the overall trend.

## Contracts measured in decades

Riot, Hut 8, TeraWulf, IREN, Bitdeer, CleanSpark, Cipher Digital, and Core Scientific signed capacity-leasing contracts with AI labs and hyperscalers this year totaling more than $70 billion — some running longer than ten years. By some estimates, up to 70% of some of these companies' revenue by the end of 2026 could come from AI contracts rather than bitcoin mining.

## What this means for the network

Public miners selling reserves doesn't directly affect mining difficulty or the network's hashrate — those are treasury decisions, not production ones. But the shift in the business model of the largest public miners — from "hold BTC" to "lease out computing capacity" — is a structural change in what public mining companies actually are: three years ago they were a proxy for the bitcoin price, today for some investors they are already a proxy for demand for AI infrastructure.
