## A change of leader

In the report on the tokenized real-world asset (RWA) market that Finality covered in early September, the only asset class called "ready for scale" was US Treasuries — with $15 billion across 100 products. But by a narrower measure — which asset class is larger excluding stablecoins — private credit overtook Treasuries back in the second quarter of 2026, and the gap has widened since.

## Who provides the volume

Platforms like Centrifuge, Maple Finance, and Goldfinch tokenize corporate loans and yield-bearing debt instruments, drawing institutional capital into a segment that is going through a rough patch in the traditional market: classic private-credit origination fell about 40% (to $44.76 billion for the quarter), and the US default rate hit a record 6%. That the on-chain segment kept growing against this backdrop isn't necessarily a sign that risks are lower there; it may also mean institutional capital is seeking yield where traditional lending channels are contracting.

## Careful with the numbers

An important caveat here, one that some outlets covering the topic omit: the counting methodologies of market trackers diverge more than in most other RWA segments. Some count only the value actually distributed among holders (about $5 billion as of March 2026), others include assets represented on platforms and locked in structures, arriving at $18–19 billion. Both figures are "right" within their own methodology — but comparing them directly, as if they were the same metric, is incorrect.

## What this means for the reader

Private-credit tokenization is a segment with less transparency than tokenized Treasuries: where Treasuries are standardized and liquid, every private loan is a separate deal with its own default risk, often without a public rating. The growth of this segment amid the weakening traditional lending market is a story to watch not just by dollar volume but by how quickly the first public defaults appear on-chain.
