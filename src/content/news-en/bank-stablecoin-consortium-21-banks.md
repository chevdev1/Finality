## From a research group to a formal consortium

A year-long research project by a group of the world's largest banks and asset managers turned into a formal consortium with real institutional backing on September 1: besides Goldman Sachs, Citi, and Bank of America, the list includes Deutsche Bank, UBS, Wells Fargo, and Fidelity Investments. The issuing company is to be established in the second half of 2026 — subject to the deal closing, which for bank consortia of this scale is always a real caveat rather than a formality.

## Whose token it is and who is behind it

The key difference from tokens like USDT or USDC: the planned stablecoin is a private liability of a separate company, backed by reserves held by the participating banks themselves, not a tokenized claim on a central bank's balance sheet. The initial focus is a dollar token for payments and digital-asset settlement, with a euro version as the next expansion step.

## Why this particular window

The launch date — the first half of 2027 — is no accident: it is aligned with January 18, 2027, when the GENIUS Act takes effect, setting reserve requirements, issuer licensing, and redemption guarantees for stablecoins in the US. The planned bank token is designed to comply with both GENIUS and Europe's MiCA where applicable — meaning it is being built from the start as a regulation-compatible instrument, not a token later retrofitted to the rules.

## What this means for the market

The total stablecoin market was estimated at roughly $302.8 billion as of September 10, of which USDT and USDC together control about 85%. A bank consortium with this lineup isn't an attempt to outgrow Tether and Circle on volume here and now, but a bid for a different niche: institutional settlement and payments, where a counterparty cares more about a regulated issuer with Goldman Sachs or Citi behind it than about wider retail distribution of the token.
