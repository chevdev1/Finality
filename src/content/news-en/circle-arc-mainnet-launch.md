## A specialized blockchain rather than a general-purpose platform

Arc isn't another universal L1/L2 network competing on TPS and fees, but a blockchain designed by the issuer of USDC specifically for stablecoin settlement. It is part of a broader trend: rather than competing for a place in the already crowded ecosystem of general-purpose blockchains, large financial players are building infrastructure for their own narrow use case.

## What's in the launch

The full product set at the September 16 launch includes transaction-privacy capabilities (details of the methodology weren't fully disclosed at the time of publication), an "agentic" stack — infrastructure that lets autonomous AI agents initiate and process payments within set limits — and support for tokenized real-world assets. Combining these three directions in one product is a direct bet that the next wave of financial activity will involve stablecoins, autonomous agents, and tokenized assets together, rather than any one of them alone.

## Part of Circle's broader expansion

Arc's launch runs in parallel with already announced partnerships: Circle is bringing USDC and EURC to Nu Global, opening access to dollar and euro balances for more than 140 million customers in over 35 countries, and has also become the title partner of Chelsea football club — a mix of infrastructure and consumer expansion at the same time.

## What to watch next

Since this is only a mainnet launch, not a report on its usage, the key questions remain open: what real transaction volume will run through Arc in the first months, whether independent stablecoin issuers or other fintech companies will build products on it besides Circle itself, and how the promised transaction "privacy" will square with regulators' transparency requirements, which are tightening for stablecoins in 2026.
