## The mainnet has become a settlement layer, not a place of activity

The growthepie figures paint a picture that has been forming over more than a quarter: the ordinary Ethereum user today almost never interacts with L1 directly. The mainnet processes about 2 million transactions a day — up from 1.5 million a year earlier, but modest next to the 25–30 million that run through L2 networks. Different trackers give slightly different exact figures (some sources cite a 13x ratio, others nearly 15x), but the direction and order of magnitude agree everywhere: the overwhelming majority of activity physically happens outside L1, which increasingly serves as a layer for final settlement and security rather than execution.

## Consolidation instead of distribution

Yet the L2 market itself isn't growing evenly across all 73 active rollups — it is consolidating around Base and Arbitrum, which together hold about 77% of the sector's [TVL](/en/glossary/#tvl) (more than $48 billion in total). Earlier this year, in June, CoinDesk wrote that not all L2s are literally dying, but many of them — especially general-purpose rollups with no clear specialization — no longer have a reason to exist as a separate network: their function has long been served by larger competitors with more liquidity and a bigger ecosystem.

## What this means for the reader

For someone evaluating a specific protocol or L2 network, this means: the mere fact of "we're an Ethereum L2" is no longer a differentiator — there are dozens of such networks, and the vast majority don't capture a meaningful share of transactions or TVL. The more important question is why a given network exists separately from Base or Arbitrum — what niche, audience, or technical solution it offers that the two market leaders don't.
