## From chatbots to autonomous traders

AI agents have become everyday market assistants in crypto, moving from simple chatbots to systems that read on-chain data around the clock, compare signals, track market sentiment, and monitor flows across DeFi, DEX, and CEX venues. Multi-agent systems split roles among specialized agents: some handle research, others risk management, others strategy optimization and execution.

## The scale is already visible on specific platforms

As of late August 2026, Olas agents had completed more than 19.1 million on-chain transactions across eight networks (96.3% of them on Gnosis). As of March 2026, Giza had processed $3.96 billion in agent-driven volume, and HeyElsa more than $300 million since launch.

## Why this deserves skepticism

Standardization around the [x402](/en/glossary/#x402-protocol) protocol and the Base network makes it easier for agents to interact with each other, but it also concentrates risk: a failure or vulnerability in either of those infrastructure layers could hit a disproportionately large share of all agent activity in crypto at once.
