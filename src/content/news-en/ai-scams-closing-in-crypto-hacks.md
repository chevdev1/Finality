## Two parallel lines of growth

The number of classic exploits in the crypto industry keeps rising — more than 207 separate cases in the first half of 2026. But a second line of threats is growing alongside it: the share of attacks where artificial intelligence is used not as a defensive tool but as part of the attack itself. By available estimates, AI use in crypto crime nearly doubled over the year — so this isn't a hypothetical future risk, it's an already measurable, accelerating trend.

## From deepfakes to autonomous extortion

One concrete example of the scale: losses from deepfake scams in 2026 have already exceeded the full-year 2025 total — meaning growth is outpacing the annual rate of the previous period. Separately, the first fully autonomous case of AI ransomware has been documented, in which, according to available data, the entire attack cycle — from reconnaissance to the extortion itself — was carried out by an AI system without step-by-step direction from a human operator at each stage.

## AI orchestration of infrastructure attacks

Also noted is a case of SecFlow, an AI orchestration framework that turns a campaign's overall goal into concrete tasks for specialized AI agents equipped with tools, target data, and network routes. That is fundamentally different from a single AI tool helping one attacker: here the AI coordinates an entire campaign, distributing work among multiple agents.

## What this means for the reader

This piece is best read alongside Finality's earlier analysis showing that key theft and infrastructure compromise have overtaken smart-contract bugs as the main cause of large DeFi losses. AI doesn't create a fundamentally new class of vulnerability here — it speeds up and scales the same attack vectors (social engineering, infrastructure compromise, fraud) that were already the industry's main problem. The difference is that such attacks used to require significant resources and time from a group of people, and AI orchestration lowers that barrier to entry.
