## What exactly was tested

U.S. Bank announced the successful execution of a live pilot transaction using USBDC — the bank's own dollar stablecoin — for a cross-border payment between the bank's divisions in North America and Europe. The transaction ran on the Stellar blockchain and, according to the bank, demonstrated the ability to move value on-chain while keeping integration with internal finance, risk, compliance, and operations systems.

## Why a bank wants its own stablecoin

The pilot tested key functions: issuance (mint), redemption, freezing, and clawback — the same set of controls as an ordinary bank account, but on a public blockchain. USBDC is one of the first stablecoins issued by a bank on a public network, which the bank describes as a way to use new technology to close gaps in the global banking system with round-the-clock transaction availability.

## What's next

Among future use cases the bank names liquidity management, collateral mobility, and cross-border treasury operations. For now USBDC isn't available to bank customers as a product — it's an infrastructure pilot, whose timing coincides with growing US regulatory interest in bank stablecoins amid discussion of market-structure bills.
