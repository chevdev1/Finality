## What Agent OS offers

Agent OS combines in one platform the exchange API, a separate hub of agent wallets, the [x402](/en/glossary/#x402-protocol) payment layer for settlements between agents, and a skills marketplace. It is built on the Model Context Protocol, an open standard from Anthropic that gives compatible AI applications a single way to connect to external tools. That's why the list of supported agents at launch includes specific products: Claude, Claude Code, Codex, ChatGPT, Cursor, and VS Code.

## Where the security boundary sits

The key protective mechanism is isolation: an authorized agent gets access to market data and can execute trades on spot, margin, conversion, and futures, but works through a sub-account with no rights to withdraw funds off the exchange. Formally, this limits the potential damage from an agent's mistake or compromise to a single sub-account.

## Why the protection is "thinner than it looks"

Industry critics note that the withdrawal restriction doesn't eliminate the risk entirely: an agent with access to futures and margin can still do real financial damage to the sub-account itself through bad or manipulative trades, even without the ability to move funds out. Binance's launch comes against a broader trend — AI bots already account, by estimates, for 58% of all crypto trading volume — and adds the largest exchange by volume to the list of venues institutionalizing agent trading rather than merely tolerating it.
