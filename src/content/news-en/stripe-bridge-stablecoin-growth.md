## From acquisition to scale

Stripe closed its purchase of Bridge, a stablecoin orchestration platform, in February 2025 for $1.1 billion. A year and a half after integration, transaction volume through the Bridge platform itself has roughly quadrupled — a concrete quantitative result showing the acquisition actually expanded use of the product rather than staying on paper.

## Who actually uses stablecoin payments

Stablecoin payment volume through Stripe in 2025 reached about $400 billion, and the structure matters here: 60% of that activity is business-to-business (B2B) settlement, not consumer retail payments. That fits the broader picture Finality has already noted in coverage of Visa's stablecoin settlement: the main practical demand for stablecoins today is corporate and cross-border settlement, where the gain in speed and cost over a traditional bank transfer is immediately felt, not retail purchases, where a card already works quickly enough.

## Cards linked to stablecoin balances

Bridge's partnership with Visa, announced earlier, lets fintech companies issue cards where settlement actually comes from a stablecoin balance rather than a traditional bank account. This partnership plans to expand to 60 countries by the third quarter of 2026 — meaning coverage is to grow by an order of magnitude in a relatively short time, unless the plan runs into regulatory delays in individual jurisdictions.

## The product line around Bridge

Beyond volume growth, Stripe has steadily built out the product wrapper around the acquisition: Open Issuance (launched in September 2025) lets any company create and manage its own stablecoin in a few lines of code, and accepting stablecoins in Stripe checkout has been enabled by default since the same month. That puts Stripe in the same category as PayPal (PYUSD) and other payment companies competing to become the infrastructure layer through which businesses issue and accept stablecoins, rather than the end issuer of a single specific token.
